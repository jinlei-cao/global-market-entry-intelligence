# Research interview guide: explain this project accurately

**Why these indicators?** They provide a transparent illustration of market demand, institutional conditions, operational connectivity and digital reach. They are only a starting point and omit industry-specific regulatory and financial details.

**Why log-transform GDP?** The absolute scale of GDP can be extremely skewed; logs reduce the influence of scale while preserving the ordering of positive values.

**Why use two ranking methods?** Weighted sum and TOPSIS operationalise decision preferences differently. Agreement offers limited reassurance about model dependence; disagreement highlights subjective assumptions.

**What does 70/100 mean?** A score of 70 is relative to the candidate set and its min–max bounds; it is not a 70% probability of successful investment.

**What is the sensitivity exercise?** It changes preference weights, ranks candidates each time and counts first-place frequencies. It is not a Monte Carlo forecast of company returns or geopolitical shocks.

**How do you handle missing data?** The baseline excludes countries lacking one or more required recent series. Median imputation is possible for a declared sensitivity scenario, but missingness may be informative and the result remains conditional.

**Why is LPI 2.0 not mixed with old LPI?** Survey-based perceptions and observed logistics tracking are different constructs. Treating them as the same series would hide a methodological break.

**What would you add for a real company?** Sector demand, buyer structure, entry requirements, ownership restrictions, tax, FX, sanctions screening, trade finance, competitive landscape, supply-chain routes, expected profitability and independent local legal diligence.

**What did the researcher personally do?** State accurately which code was reviewed, run, changed or interpreted personally. This project begins from an AI-assisted scaffold; independently reproduce real-data results before describing the project as self-directed work.
